# Scan batches

**Take photo** opens the [PakTrak camera](CAMERA.md) with framing guides, bottom capture controls, optional hardware light/zoom and review/retake before **Upload & scan**. **Phone camera** preserves native full-resolution capture and **Library** opens saved photos. Camera preview and controls are local; identification starts on the server after upload.

Photos default to zero foils. If any cards are foil (including etched foil), enter their count, then upload a still photo with separated, face-up cards and visible edges. After acceptance, the upload form resets to zero foils for the next photo. Once PakTrak confirms acceptance, the phone can disconnect. Docker workers prepare the photo, find card outlines, read names and collector text, search the local English catalog, and compare candidate artwork with cached catalog images. No phone OCR, paid inference service, or upload of private photos to a third-party recognizer is required.

Uploads support JPEG, PNG, WebP, HEIC/HEIF, AVIF, TIFF, BMP and still GIF. The worker selects the primary HEIC/HEIF photo, handles rotation and embedded color profiles, and creates metadata-free JPEG working images. See [photo formats](PHOTO_FORMATS.md) for limits and multi-image behavior.

Borderless and full-art detection combines color boundaries with the existing grayscale and local-lighting passes. Two edge scales and bounded convex-hull recovery help follow weak or interrupted outlines; contour fill, visible-side checks, card proportions and overlap suppression reject unsupported shapes and nested artwork. Before choosing large outlines, the detector checks for two distinct, similarly sized card outlines filling the same area and reaching its outside corners. This prevents an outline around neighboring cards from suppressing the individual cards, while retaining a single card containing inset artwork panels. This is a CPU image-processing improvement, not a newly trained model. Keep space between cards and include their full edges; blended edges, glare and overlapping cards may still need **Add a missed card** or **Adjust crop**.

**Batches** opens a paginated list with a small card preview strip, date, card/import counts, price estimate and processing or review status for each photo. Batches awaiting card approval or finish confirmation have a highlighted **Ready for your review** row with a **Review batch** prompt. Open a row to see that batch's overview; the list is replaced, so there is only one batch open at a time. **Back to batches** at the top and **Close batch** at the bottom return to the list. Opening Batches from the menu also returns to the list. The upload screen also offers **Review saved batches**, and new accepted uploads open their own overview automatically.

The **Finish your batch** section at the top shows two steps: choose foil cards, then review remaining card matches. **Choose foil cards** opens the selection directly; **Review card matches** opens the first pending match. The shortcuts scroll and move keyboard focus to the relevant section, respect reduced-motion preferences, and preserve unfinished selections. Saved steps show checkmarks; zero-foil batches already have their nonfoil labels and need no foil confirmation. Processing batches explain when foil selection will become available. Every batch opens ready for review; there is no separate edit mode to switch on. Approvals, saved card corrections and confirmed finishes persist immediately on the server; there is no separate whole-batch save. The saved/unsaved indicator distinguishes accepted changes from unfinished form inputs. Leaving unfinished card, foil or crop choices requires confirmation; cancelling keeps the inputs. Leaving does not approve unresolved cards. Saved decisions and pending suggestions remain available on reopening. Navigation waits for an active save, and the browser is asked to warn before unloading unsaved edits (mobile browsers may not show unload prompts).

The batch shows cropped cards, suggested printings, matching evidence, progress and a measured processing-time estimate. Open a card to compare its crop with the catalog image. **Previous** and **Next** browse every card from a bottom navigation bar, with phone safe-area spacing and large touch targets. The bar stays in view while you scroll a card and holds **Approve & import** (or **Approve match** for deck scans) between the arrows, so a phone review is a thumb tap per card. **Next to review** beside the card actions skips imported/ignored cards. The counter uses the visible card order, including batches with gaps in region indexes. **Approve & import** adds one copy and opens the next pending card; **Edit card / printing** searches all printings and exposes the existing set, rarity, collector-number and language filters. Imported cards can be corrected without losing their source association. **Select suggestions** and **Import selected** offer a separate finish, condition and storage-location choice for the selected cards. Repeated physical copies remain separate source lots and accumulate in the collection's quantity display.

In **Find an exact printing**, type a card name followed by its collector number, such as `Plains #287`. This works for every card type, including letter or symbol suffixes such as `#287a`. The number must match exactly; `#287` does not include `#1287` or `#287a`. Numbers can repeat across sets, so use **Set / expansion** to narrow the edition and check the result before approving. The separate Collector number field reflects the shortcut; changing that field replaces it, and **Clear printing filters** removes it while keeping the name. These searches use the server's saved catalog.

**View photo and card outlines**, **Add a missed card**, and **Adjust crop** allow count and geometry checks. Tap around the four corners starting anywhere, in either direction, or drag an existing corner. Each corner has a finger-sized handle; a corner moves with your finger from wherever you grab it rather than jumping underneath it, and a round magnifier shows the area around the corner at the top of the photo, on the side away from your finger, with a crosshair and the outline edges. Arrow-key adjustments show the magnifier too. A touch that turns into scrolling does not leave a stray corner behind. The server puts the corners in a consistent order before cropping, preventing tap order from flipping or mirroring the result. Focused corners support arrow keys. **Check photo again** re-detects and identifies remaining review cards; geometry overlap preserves existing observation IDs, imported copies and manually drawn outlines. The expected-count control starts at 15 and is only a review aid. The detector does not invent cards to reach that number. Its 32-region cap is a resource limit.

Card photos in review, galleries, foil selection and batch previews use the orientation selected during recognition. This also applies to existing crops with a saved recognition rotation. If a photo still needs correcting, choose **Flip photo 180°** below **Your photo**. The orientation saves immediately and follows the card after reopening. Pending cards are identified again on the server using that explicit orientation; the automatic flip check cannot undo it. Flipping an imported or ignored card only changes its displayed photo. It does not add copies, change the saved printing or consume another guest scan. Rechecking a batch preserves manually selected orientations; drawing a new outline resets orientation for the newly generated crop.

## Reading names and printed codes

Each card is read from its title strip and its bottom-left printed code (for example `171/269 C · AKH • EN` or `R 0282 · INR • EN`).

- **Sleeved cards.** Sleeves and loose outlines leave a margin above the card, so its title sits lower in the crop. When the usual strip has no confident name, two lower strips are read, for both orientations when the orientation is automatic. A card is only turned 180° when the upside-down reading is a plausible name, so junk text cannot flip an upright card.
- **Printed codes.** Set codes containing digits (M15, C18, MH3) count as set evidence. Common footer misreads are tolerated: O/Q/D for 0, I/L for 1, S for 5, B for 8, a rarity letter run into the number (`RO380`), and a trailing smudge after the set code (`IMAce`). Copyright years are ignored.
- **Code-only suggestions.** When the title is unreadable, a readable set code and collector number together suggest that exact printing. A collector number without a set code only suggests cards whose title text is at least half similar. These suggestions need corroborating artwork or a similar title, and an unreadable title keeps them below the automatic-import threshold.
- **Short last words.** Short trailing words that are usually mana symbols are still tried as part of an exact name, so cards like **Bulk Up** match.
- **Light title text.** Old black frames, showcase and borderless cards print light text on a dark title strip. A dark strip is also read inverted, which Tesseract handles far more reliably. The usual read is kept too, since a loose crop can darken an ordinary title strip.
- **Reprinted names.** When the printed code is unreadable, a name with many printings also has one printing of each other artwork compared (up to eight artworks in all), instead of only the first few printings in catalog order. Printings that share an illustration still need the set and collector number to tell apart. Automatic import still needs printed-code evidence.
- **Printed code.** The footer is read twice, once in a slightly wider area, so a set code whose first letter sits at the edge of a tight crop is still read whole.
- **Speed.** Both title strips and the printed code are read at the same time. Fuzzy name search compares the title with the 2,000 catalog names that share the most letter pairs instead of every name, and remembers titles already searched. Artwork features of recently compared catalog images are kept in memory. The worker rereads the catalog only after a catalog update.

In a development comparison on 251 owner-confirmed crops from saved batches, the 133 cards that had needed manual review went from 66 to 99 correct top printings in standard mode (73 to 103 with enhanced scanning). Cards with no suggestion dropped from 49 to 15 (41 to 11). With enhanced scanning, 40 of them would now import automatically. The one that differed from the saved printing was checked against its photo, which shows the suggested printing, so the earlier approval had been wrong. The 118 previously auto-imported cards were unchanged or improved (115 to 117 correct in standard mode, 117 in both enhanced runs). Recognition takes about 0.2 seconds longer per card at the median; the 95th percentile roughly doubles (about 3 seconds standard, 6 enhanced) for cards that need the extra strips. This is a development measurement on one collection, not a held-out accuracy benchmark.

## Optional enhanced scanning

Administrators can enable **Menu → Administration → Scan processing → Enhanced scanning**. The setting is saved for the whole server and defaults to **off**, including upgraded installations. It requires no GPU, model download, paid service or phone processing. Switching it changes upcoming card-identification steps without restarting Docker; a card already being processed finishes using its previous setting. Saved collection cards are not reimported by changing the switch. Use **Check photo again** to retry pending cards in a saved batch.

The normal 600×840 crop and recognition result remain the baseline. When that result needs help, the worker temporarily makes a 1200×1680 crop from the saved prepared photo, then resizes the text strips with mild noise reduction, local contrast adjustment and sharpening. Larger crops are created only for the extra pass and are not stored. The original upload, prepared photo, card outlines and saved preview crops stay intact. Existing batches and manual outlines use this same path. This is CPU image preprocessing, not generative reconstruction of unreadable text.

The extra result is accepted only if it improves the top match score. A different suggested printing also needs equal or stronger name evidence and corroborating artwork evidence. Ties and weaker results retain the baseline. Strong, unambiguous normal matches skip extra work. Failed photo reads, crop preparation, OCR or an exhausted extra-pass budget fall back to the normal result. The extra pass gets an eight-second budget with shorter OCR/storage timeouts; slow baseline processing skips it to leave room within the existing worker deadline. The automatic-import threshold remains **strictly greater than 88% match strength**.

Enhanced scanning uses more CPU, working memory and private-storage reads. The job stage names the mode while reading a card, and the existing measured progress estimate includes the added time. In a local comparison of 27 cards across three saved development photos, standard mode reproduced the previous recognizer's output exactly. The optional pass improved two weak/missing card-name suggestions without weakening any top score in that run. The fifteen-card photo took about 10 seconds of recognition with standard processing and 20 seconds with enhancement; the other photos took roughly 3→4 and 5→12 seconds. These are warm-cache timings on this development server, excluding queue/upload time and measured without repeated source-photo reads; they are not device-independent estimates or held-out accuracy results. Shared-art editions and unreadable text still need review. Private evidence is in ignored `artifacts/enhanced-scanning/`.

## Confidence and automatic additions

The displayed percentage is **match strength**, a retrieval score combining name similarity, printed identifiers, optional set evidence from the batch, and artwork feature agreement. It is not a calibrated probability of a correct printing. Shared artwork, unreadable collector numbers, tokens and unusual layouts can remain ambiguous. Condition stays **ungraded** unless the owner chooses it.

**Owner direction, September 19, 2026 supersedes the earlier qualification prerequisite:** a valid server-produced candidate with match strength **strictly greater than 0.88** imports automatically. Exactly 88%, absent/invalid scores, non-matches and unsupported selected finishes remain for review. The worker records the score, threshold, policy version and model in the automatic-add event; the gallery labels these copies **Auto-imported**. Browser-supplied scores cannot activate the rule. Calibration and a held-out photographic evaluation remain future validation work; 88% match strength does not mean measured 88% accuracy.

## Foil and nonfoil labels

The upload form asks **How many cards are foil?** Zero means all cards in that photo are nonfoil, including any regions added later, with no foil selection required. New API uploads with an omitted/null count also default to zero; existing upload receipts keep their original interpretation. A positive count leaves finishes unknown during initial server processing until the user selects the foil cards. Older batches without a recorded count keep their existing labels. Finish selection never blocks server identification or automatic additions, so the phone may disconnect as soon as the upload is accepted.

After identification, **Choose foil cards** in the next steps or **Select foil cards** in the editor opens a crop gallery. The foil section has a distinct heading and accent border, larger two-column cards on narrow phones, selection checkmarks, and a selected/expected progress counter. The counter and **Confirm card finishes** stay near the bottom while scrolling the foil section. Tap exactly the declared number, correcting the count if needed, then confirm. Selected cards become foil (or etched foil using the foil-type selector); every other active card becomes nonfoil. Setting the count to zero clears foil selections and lets the user confirm all cards as nonfoil without individually deselecting them. The gallery appears only for a positive count. This updates already-imported source copies, pending cards and price estimates without adding quantities or moving storage locations. Known catalog finish conflicts must be corrected before the whole selection can save. Ignored non-card regions are excluded. **Change foil cards** allows later correction, and individual card correction still works. Once finishes are saved, **Next: review matches** leads to the first card still needing approval.

The foil plan and per-card labels persist in PostgreSQL. A token guards against overwriting changes from another session; the endpoint validates count, membership, printing finishes and request receipts under the same scan locks used by recognition/deletion. Finish changes receive zero-quantity audit events. Bulk approval defaults to keeping each card’s own finish. Once the declared number of foils has been selected, cards added by a recheck or manual outline default to nonfoil. If the selections are incomplete or no longer match the declared count, new regions stay unknown until reviewed; current imported-copy finishes take precedence over earlier observation labels.

**Cards in this photo** can be cleared while typing a replacement count. Leaving it blank restores the detected card count (or 15 if no cards are detected); its range remains 1–32. It is a local review aid, not a target that changes detection or adds copies.

## Batch value

Batch estimates update as suggestions and approvals arrive, using the saved TCGplayer, Card Kingdom or ManaPool price feed. Candidate printings are provisional. Unknown finishes use a range across available finish quotes, without changing the stored finish. An explicit finish uses that finish's quote. Missing matches/prices are counted separately, and the feed update time is shown. Estimates are not purchase prices or guaranteed sale proceeds.

## Scans for decks

**Decks → Scan a deck** and a saved deck’s **Scan cards** action link multiple photos to a deck. These scans save reviewed identities without adding collection copies by default; upload offers an explicit option to add copies too. Automatic match strength, foil review, server processing, scan quotas and correction tools still apply. A deck preview combines selected reviewed cards, assigns sections, and adds each source card once. See [physical deck scanning](DECKS.md#scanning-a-physical-deck).

## Deletion and integrity

**Delete batch**, at the bottom of every batch, first shows the number of remaining source copies and a confirmation explaining that the photo batch, its ongoing work and those collection copies will be removed. Copies moved into another binder or corrected to another printing still belong to their original scan. Copies from other scans/imports remain. Saved deck lists remain and their availability/shortages update. Deleting or ignoring regions never restores a guest's lifetime allowance.

Deletion locks owner → job → scan → source lots, checks a confirmation token against the current lot versions/counts, records negative inventory events, zeroes only that batch's remaining lots, and marks a persistent deletion barrier. Repeated deletion is harmless. Workers check the barrier and their lease before writing; stale workers cannot add cards after deletion. Private endpoints hide the deleted batch immediately. Retention maintenance removes its referenced objects on its next pass (normally within a minute); unreferenced upload/worker-attempt objects retain the existing 24-hour orphan grace to avoid deleting an ambiguously committed object. Audit records remain in the database.

Recognition checkpoints one card per durable job step. Successful checkpoints reset the consecutive-failure count, retain the overall deadline, and queue the next step through the outbox. Each task retains the 45/60-second soft/hard limits and 90-second lease. A crash resumes pending cards; an edited crop invalidates an old recognition result. Manual crop edits have replay receipts and do not charge another scan. New missed regions are charged once and respect the 100-card lifetime guest quota.

## Evidence and limits

The September 20 adjacent-card case contains eight physical cards, but the previous detector chose seven regions because an enclosing outline suppressed two individually visible cards. The updated selection finds eight whole-card regions; the earlier four-card borderless and fifteen-card photos retain their counts and outlines. Four original synthetic scenes reproduce the previous merge, including horizontal/vertical neighbors, rotation, narrow gaps and perspective. Whole-card geometry checks now pass, alongside negative cases with two large inset artwork panels in one card. Integration checks exercise rechecking a pending merged region into two cards at both below- and above-threshold match strengths: the original observation ID is retained, only one extra scan is charged, and repeated rechecks do not duplicate regions or copies. Private development images remain in ignored `artifacts/adjacent-cards/` and are not distributed as training data or fixtures.

Development inspection of one owner-supplied 3024×4032 photo found 15 visible cards. The old detector proposed 12 regions. The adaptive detector proposed all 15, and the OCR/artwork matcher returned the correct card name first for all 15 in the inspected run. Shared-art printing alternatives remained visible. This is a development case used while improving the implementation, not a held-out accuracy benchmark or a supported-device/count certification. A recheck exposed an OpenCV intersection-tolerance issue at unit-square scale; intersections now use pixel-scale coordinates to avoid treating an almost identical outline as a second physical card. A regression test protects this behavior. The private image, crops, output and screenshots remain in ignored local artifacts and are not distributed as fixtures.

The September 20 borderless case contains four cards. The grayscale/adaptive detector found three; the color-aware detector finds four, including the previously missed borderless card. The earlier 15-card photo still yields exactly 15. `tests/test_borderless_detection.py` uses original synthetic artwork to verify whole-card intersection-over-union, rotated layouts, 15 mixed bordered/borderless cards, downscaling, perspective and rejection of blank/noisy tables, non-card shapes and large concavities. Recheck integration cases verify that a newly detected card is charged once, below-threshold suggestions remain pending, above-threshold suggestions import once, and repeated rechecks preserve existing imported copies and outlines. These are development regressions, not measured general recognition accuracy. Private evaluation photos stay under ignored `artifacts/borderless/`.

`tests/test_scan_batches.py` covers source-specific deletion, stale confirmations, deletion during recognition, disconnected processing, idempotent approvals, crop revisions/replays, guest quotas, batch value ranges, the strict 88% match-strength policy, full-name retrieval and 15 distinct synthetic regions. `tests/test_scan_finishes.py` also checks pre-upload counts, mixed automatic/manual decisions, finish compatibility, atomic updates, stale snapshots, retries and unchanged source quantities. Browser tests cover Previous/Next navigation, draft preservation, automatic review advancement, foil selection, the gallery, approval, correction, bulk import, deletion warnings, progress and crop controls at phone/desktop sizes in Chromium and WebKit. Synthetic fixtures contain no real card art or holdings. Physical-phone capture tests, a representative held-out photographic corpus, calibrated printing confidence and targeted replacement close-up uploads remain outstanding.

OCR uses the packaged Tesseract English model with bounded subprocesses; preprocessing follows [Tesseract's image-quality guidance](https://tesseract-ocr.github.io/tessdoc/ImproveQuality.html) and [command-line documentation](https://tesseract-ocr.github.io/tessdoc/Command-Line-Usage.html). Reference images are fetched only through the existing allowlisted catalog cache; no per-card metadata/price API request is made while scanning.
